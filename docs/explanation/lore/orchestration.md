**The name.** A loom is where the warp is strung before the weft goes through:
the plan and its dependencies laid out first, many threads becoming one cloth
under **one weaver**. The image carries the constraint that matters — the split
is cheap, and the rejoining is the work.

## The mechanism

This subject owns the assignment rather than the act: which familiar a task is
given to, with which model, which tools, which permissions, and how a task is
divided and put back together. Two neighbours are excluded by definition, and the
register's criteria say so explicitly — a harness or a model *changing* is the
[Guises](harness.md), and what a procedure *says* is the
[Apothecary](skills.md).

## The design choice, and why

**One weaver.** Work may be split across agents; authority over the result is
not. That is the same rule the rest of the system applies to models — a familiar
brings back what it found, and a human decides what may be kept — applied one
scale up, where the risk is not a bad claim but an unaccountable merge.

**The subject was ratified before its mechanism.** The register records the
decision (2026-10-12) and the criteria, and the delegation question itself is
still open. Declaring the subject first is deliberate: a place for the answer,
occupied by nothing yet, is the register's own way of saying the answer is not
known — an empty subject is *declared*, never missing.

## Lineage

**No source claimed** for the name; the register is mnemonic. The technical
lineage is in the harness leaves, which describe what the existing delegation
mechanisms actually do, and in the open question left standing beside them.

**Where the analogy stops.** A loom is a craft with a practised hand; the
mechanism here is assignment, permission and merge discipline, which is
bookkeeping rather than skill. The name also overstates the current state: the
threads exist, and the cloth is the part still being decided.
